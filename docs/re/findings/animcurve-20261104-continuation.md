# Animation-curve continuation, 2026-11-04

## Accepted semantic correction

`detail::InterpolateCurveU8` had the cubic coefficient subtraction reversed. The correct expression is `t * (t * (c0 + c1 - 2.0f))`. The previous expression used `2.0f - (c0 + c1)`. This correction follows the original executable's operand values; it is not a fake match or a claimed exact-function gain.

The frozen `orig/wpse-v1/main.dol` contains `40000000`, or `2.0f`, at virtual address `0x804BA650`, DOL file offset `0x3E65F0`. In the original `AnimCurveExecuteU8` object:

| Function offset | Original instruction | Proven value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `0x754` | `lfs f8, lbl_804BA650` | `f8 = 2.0f` |
| `0x84C` | `fadds f7, f12, f9` | `f7 = c0 + c1` |
| `0x860` | `fsubs f7, f7, f8` | `f7 = c0 + c1 - 2.0f` |

The coefficient registers come from the two type-bit selections immediately preceding the addition. Their literals are `0.0f` at `0x804BA628` and `1.5f` at `0x804BA654`. The other polynomial constants are `3.0f` at `0x804BA64C` and `-2.0f` at `0x804BA658`. The float interpolation helper already uses the correct subtraction order.

An isolated include overlay containing only this correction preserves all eight function scores, including the three functions exact at that point. The coordinator applied the one-line correction to `include/nw4r/ef/ef_animcurveinternal.h`; five executors were then nonexact. Later exact-function gains are recorded below.

### Integer allocation and final scheduling cause

The timing process flag originally survived the first simplify pass at degree 31. Its early coloring opened saved register `r22` before the key-search and hash temporaries were colored, causing the apparently unrelated later rotations. Three timing neighbors can instead be removed before it: the normal period, the fitted loop-count load, and the product of fitted loop and period. An ordinary inline normal-period local supplies the first. The latter two require caller-owned output references: a same-type `u32` reassignment after the loop-count load, and separate assignment followed by `*=` for the product. A direct product initializer folds back to an anonymous expression and fails to preserve the desired node.

Keeping the process flag as an ordinary inline local then gives it the necessary position between the later hash temporaries and the key-search temporaries. The fitted period remains a caller-owned reference, declared after the two low-degree neighbors so that its own first simplify visit still occurs while they are active. This preserves its target `r28` and restores the saved-register assignments, all hash temporaries, and the upper search frame. A one-field `FloatCurveKeyMatch` output preserves the remaining exact-result/lower-bound priority. The earlier declaration leaves the seed multiplier in `r4`, the type in `r5`, the range pointer in `r6`, and the channel bit in `r7`.

Only the instructions at function offsets `0x724` and `0x728` then remained reversed. A read-only scheduler hook and native compiler disassembly showed a final ready-list tie: both nodes have the same deadline, depth, pending dependency count, and successor-unlock count. ## Traps

- **Masked register comparisons can conceal reversed subtraction.** U8's incorrect `2.0f - (c0 + c1)` had the same opcode sequence and fuzzy score as the correct expression because both operand registers already differed. Track the values feeding noncommutative operations and verify their relocation-backed literals before treating a remaining operand difference as allocation alone.
- **An identity control depends on the surrounding inline structure.** The `void*` reassignment which retains a computed receiver elsewhere did not repair Texture's original helper-based form. After expanding the frame calculation, several pointer identities preserve the required local, and moving the fitted-period declaration closes the final register pair. Check surviving nodes and simplify order before treating an earlier negative identity experiment as universal.
- **Late cached values can alter an earlier register assignment.** F32's incorrect stride registers originate in high-degree cached values in its final random-key branch. The allocator trace, not the early instruction neighborhood, identifies those blockers. Naming the cached flags, index, and shared seed sum restores the earlier setup. The accepted F32 function verifies this local control; it does not establish a general recipe for every register rotation.
- **A high initial degree can still simplify early.** F32's conversion bias begins with degree 39 but reaches its turn with working degree 28 because eleven earlier neighbors have been removed. Delaying four proven neighbors restores the target floating-point allocation. Inspect the removal sequence and surviving graph, not just the initial degree or declaration order.
- **An early saved-register allocation can affect later nonoverlapping temporaries.** The degree-31 timing flag opened `r22` too early and caused the F32 search/hash rotations. Preserving three earlier removable neighbors, then keeping the flag inline-local, restored those later assignments without changing their arithmetic.
- **A coalesced pointer copy can still determine scheduling order.** F32's final two-word swap is a ready-list tie.
